## `Hello world!`

Thanks for reaching this corner of the site. I write code for a living, but I'm a human first — so I also do things that don't involve a keyboard.

Husband, dog parent, curious reader, and (more recently) an associate instructor at the International Academy of Holokinetic Psychology. More on all of that below.

## I am happily married to _Xochitl_

The photo above is the two of us. We've been together for a while now, and yes — we're kind of cute.

What the photo doesn't show is how we got here. That story involves a territorial dog named Bombon, and it's coming up below.

## The dog saga

We love dogs. There's a running joke about their names: except for the two Pulgas (mother and daughter, both *flea* in Spanish), every dog in our life has been named after a Mexican sweet or candy. The joke started by accident and became a tradition.

What follows is the story of how eight dogs came to share our home — and the four who couldn't stay.

### Era 1: before Xochitl

It started with *Pulga 1*, a small dog who was part of my life long before I met my wife. In the summer of 2014, Pulga 1 had a litter of three puppies: *Bombon*, *Miel* (honey), and *Turrón* (a Mexican nougat). Three sweet names right out of the gate — the tradition chose itself.

*Turrón* didn't make it to adulthood. He was still a puppy when he died, and his loss taught me a hard lesson I hadn't expected at that age: not everyone sees animals as family. Some people see puppies as cute objects, as toys — and that has consequences. It wasn't our fault, but it marked me.

*Miel* is still alive and well. She lives in another city with my mother, where she continues her lifelong mission of pulling pranks on her.

*Pulga 1* herself passed in the spring of 2019. I never found out exactly what happened. She was the first dog I ever lost — and the original exception to the sweet-name rule.

### How Bombon chose Xochitl

Bombon was the heart of the house. Hyperactive, grumpy, affectionate, energetic, and feisty — sometimes all at once. Depending on the mood, we called her *Bombota*, *Bombotita*, *Bombosa*, or *Bombonota*. Marshmallow by name, firecracker by nature.

She was also fiercely territorial. She bit people. So when I started dating Xochitl, I gave her a heads-up: *be careful with Bombon, she bites.* What I didn't say out loud was that I'd already made up my mind: if Bombon didn't accept her, the relationship wasn't going anywhere. The dogs were non-negotiable.

What happened next is something I'll never forget. Within minutes of meeting, Bombon was playing with Xochitl's hair, and Xochitl was hugging and kissing her like they were old friends. No growling. No hesitation. Just instant trust.

That was the moment I thought: *maybe she's the one.*

Bombon stayed by my side through every chapter of my adult life — before Xochitl, the courtship, the marriage, and eleven and a half years of everything else. She passed on January 5, 2026. She gave us everything until the very last day. We miss her.

### Era 2: with Xochitl

By the time Xochitl and I started our life together, I had three dogs: Bombon, *Chocorol*, and *Oreo*.

*Chocorol* — the big male of the house, named after a popular Mexican chocolate cake roll (a Swiss roll you can find almost anywhere in Mexico). I also call him *Don Checho*. Still with us.

*Oreo* was Chocorol's littermate. Named after the cookie, obviously. We eventually gave him up for adoption to someone who loved him dearly and cared for him. We lost track of him over the years, but we know he was happy.

After Oreo left, Xochitl adopted *Candi* — *hard candy*, also known around the house as *Mamachita*. Sweet name, strong personality.

Then, through our own carelessness, Candi and Chocorol had a litter of five puppies. We kept them all — and sterilized both parents and every puppy right after:

- *Bubulubu* (F) — named after another Mexican classic: a marshmallow-and-jellybean sandwich covered in chocolate. A.k.a. *Buba*.
- *Winnis* (F) — a soft candy popular on the Mexican border.
- *Cookies* (F) — because cookies are delicious. A.k.a. *Cuquis* or *Cuca*.
- *Tutsi* (F) — the Mexican Cherry Tootsie Pop.
- *Cremino* (M) — a cream-filled sweet, and the only boy of the litter.

In 2021, parvovirus hit us. Winnis, Cookies, and Cremino caught it. Winnis and Cookies pulled through. Cremino didn't. He was barely a puppy. It was brutal.

A couple of years later, Xochitl found two puppies abandoned in a shoebox in a vacant lot, in the middle of summer. She rescued both. We kept one and named her *Pulga* — in honor of Bombon's mother, the original. A tiny Chihuahua, true to her name.

Then, in August 2023 — at the peak of a 50 °C summer — someone left a puppy at our door. He couldn't have been more than three months old; his baby teeth were still coming in. We named him *Macarron*.

Over time, we discovered Macarron has idiopathic epilepsy: seizures with no identifiable medical cause, apparently triggered by stress. At one point, we came close to putting him to sleep. The episodes were relentless.

But Macarron is one of those dogs who knows how to make himself understood — he's sharp, present, and quietly intelligent. After one especially hard seizure, I sat with him and looked him straight in the eyes, in **Unitary Perception** — a state of mind I'll explain in the next section. The only thing that surfaced in me, clearly and unmistakably, was three words: *I want to live.*

We never considered euthanasia again. He's been on daily medication ever since, and he's part of the family.

## Things I care about

I'm drawn to four threads that, to me, are the same thread seen from different angles: **science, technology, religion, and politics**. Not as separate disciplines competing for attention, but as tools to bring humans closer together instead of pushing them apart.

Lately that interest has a name. I'm an **associate instructor at the International Academy of Holokinetic Psychology** <!-- TODO: verify canonical URL — candidate: https://psicologiaholokinetica.org -->.

You just read what Unitary Perception feels like in practice — that moment with Macarron after his seizure. Here's the formal version: **Unitary Perception** is the simultaneous awareness of everything that is happening, without the constant filter of interpretation. A state of mind in which the whole is perceived at once, before thought divides it into parts.

My long-term plan is to dedicate more time to this — ideally full-time — and, if life allows, to do some scientific research aligned with the **non-particularist vision of science** proposed by the physicist [David Bohm](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qm-bohm/), whose work on *wholeness and the implicate order* is the philosophical ground underneath everything above.
